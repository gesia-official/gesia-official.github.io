01 TECHNOLOGY / 01

AI Needs More Than Intelligence.

AI needs operating structures beyond model intelligence.

Language models understand natural language, reason and generate content. Real organizational work requires more: whose work is this, what must be achieved, which knowledge should be used, what is permitted, and which decisions require approval?

GESIA builds infrastructure that answers these questions, connecting model reasoning to practical individual and organizational execution capability.

02 TECHNOLOGY / 02

Common Context

A Shared Foundation for Intelligence.

Structuring shared context for AI.

Common Context connects identity, goals, knowledge, experience and working environments so AI can understand and use them. It aims to make relevant context accessible even when work and knowledge are distributed across documents and systems, maintaining consistency across agents and services.

Core components

• Identity & Role: individual and organizational identity and roles

• Goal & Task Context: goals and work context

• Knowledge & Experience: expertise and accumulated experience

• Shared Context: common context available across AI agents

03 TECHNOLOGY / 03

Governance Context

Autonomy With Accountability.

An AI decision and execution environment with authority and responsibility.

As AI use expands, verifiability, accountability and control matter alongside autonomy. Governance Context structures roles, authority, approvals, responsibilities and recording and verification rules. It distinguishes autonomous tasks from those requiring human judgment or approval and aims to make evidence and outcomes traceable.

Core components

• Authority: permissions and access scope

• Approval: approval and hold conditions

• Accountability: roles and responsibilities

• Audit & Verification: recording, tracing and outcome verification

AI should be capable of action and accountable for its execution process.

04 TECHNOLOGY / 04

Knowledge–Action Chain

From Knowledge to Executable Skills.

A structure connecting knowledge to action.

GESIA structures the transformation of human and organizational knowledge into execution capability through the Knowledge–Action Chain.

Identity → Goal → Task → Knowledge → Method → Skill

Identity defines whom intelligence serves. Goal sets the objective; Task breaks it into concrete work. Knowledge connects necessary information, and Method defines how work is performed. Skill turns these into executable capabilities AI can use. This is a core design concept for transforming expertise into reusable AI execution assets.

05 TECHNOLOGY / 05

Intelligence That Can Decide and Act.

Beyond generation, toward decisions and execution.

We research structures that help AI decide whether to act, hold or seek clarification based on the situation and criteria. This connects interpretation of goals and conditions, reference to relevant knowledge, decision criteria, permission checks and outcome verification. AI judgment takes place within human-defined criteria and responsibilities.

Understand → Reason → Decide → Act

06 TECHNOLOGY / 06

Every Action Becomes an Opportunity to Learn.

Verify outcomes and feed them back into knowledge.

Completing a task does not establish that an AI outcome is valid or trustworthy. We prioritize structures that verify and record evidence, process and results. Verified outcomes can inform improvements to existing knowledge and working methods.

Learning here does not necessarily mean automatic model weight updates. It includes improving knowledge, rules, skills, methods and evaluation criteria during operations and reflecting those improvements in subsequent execution.

Action → Verification → Feedback → Improvement
